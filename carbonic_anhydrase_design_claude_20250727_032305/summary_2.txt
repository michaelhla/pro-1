# Carbonic Anhydrase Engineering Summary

## Main Objective
Design more stable carbonic anhydrase variants while maintaining catalytic activity for improved carbon capture applications.

## Design Strategies Tested

1. **Rubredoxin Domain Insertion with Optimized Linkers**
   - Inserted 54-aa P. furiosus rubredoxin at position 169
   - Used optimized flexible linkers (GGGGSGGGGSGGGGS)
   - Rosetta score: 875.53 REU
   - Compact structure (radius of gyration: 23.87 Å)
   - Catalytic site integrity maintained (RMSD: 0.176)
   - Secondary structure: 11.0% helix, 24.6% sheet, 64.4% loop

2. **Random Disulfide Bond Introduction**
   - Added disulfides in both carbonic anhydrase and rubredoxin domains
   - Rosetta score: 894.10 REU
   - Radius of gyration: 23.04 Å
   - Catalytic RMSD: 0.164
   - Secondary structure: 11.0% helix, 22.9% sheet, 66.1% loop

3. **Interface-Stabilizing Disulfide Bonds**
   - Strategic disulfide placement at domain interfaces
   - Improved Rosetta score: 776.05 REU
   - Most compact structure (radius of gyration: 22.91 Å)
   - Maintained catalytic geometry (RMSD: 0.171)
   - Secondary structure: 11.0% helix, 24.6% sheet, 64.4% loop

## Key Insights
- The rubredoxin insertion provides stability without compromising catalytic function
- Interface-stabilizing disulfides created the most compact and stable design
- All variants maintained excellent positioning of catalytic residues

## Recommended Design
The interface-stabilizing disulfide variant offers the best properties with lowest energy score (776.05 REU) and most compact structure.
