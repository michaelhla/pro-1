# Carbonic Anhydrase Engineering Project Summary

## Project Objective
Design thermostable carbonic anhydrase variants while maintaining catalytic function.

## Key Design Approaches Tested

### Bold Design (First Attempt)
- Incorporated EP domain from plant ferritin (24aa, from soybean seed H-2 ferritin)
- Added zinc knuckle domain (18aa) at position 175
- Added N-terminal stabilizing sequence
- **Results**: 
  - Rosetta score: 331.07 REU (positive/unstable)
  - Very high RMSD values (15.04Å overall, 13.31Å active site)
  - Catalytic machinery significantly disrupted
  - Structurally compact but energetically unfavorable

### Refined Conservative Design
- Added zinc finger motif (CPECGKSFSQSSN) near C-terminus
- Maintained most of the original structure (95% sequence identity)
- **Results**:
  - Rosetta score: 160.18 REU (improved but still positive)
  - Excellent active site preservation (RMSD: 0.18Å)
  - Near-perfect zinc binding site preservation (RMSD: 0.007Å)
  - Overall RMSD with reference: 4.72Å (acceptable)
  - Very compact structure (radius of gyration: 17.38Å)
  - Good secondary structure content (17.2% helix, 25.3% sheet)

### Thermostable Design v1
- Added N-terminal propeptide inspired by thermolysin-like proteases
- Inserted zinc-binding module from thermostable proteins
- Introduced strategic cysteine pairs for disulfide formation
- **Results**:
  - Rosetta score: 685.52 REU (highly unstable)
  - Very high RMSD values for active site (12.18Å) and zinc binding (11.65Å)
  - Catalytic machinery significantly disrupted

### Thermostable Design v2
- More conservative approach with focused domain insertion
- Added CAACPVCAL zinc-binding motif near C-terminus
- **Results**: Not fully evaluated (process in progress)

## Key Findings
1. Bold, large domain insertions (24+ amino acids) significantly disrupted the protein structure and catalytic machinery
2. Strategic, smaller insertions of zinc-binding motifs showed better preservation of structure
3. The refined conservative design showed the best balance of innovation and functionality
4. Active site preservation is critical - designs with low active site RMSD values are most promising

## Current Status
The refined conservative design (with zinc finger motif near C-terminus) shows the most promise for further development, with excellent catalytic site preservation and reasonable overall structure.

## Next Steps
Continue iterating with more focused modifications that maintain the active site geometry while enhancing stability through:
1. Disulfide bond engineering at non-critical regions
2. Smaller domain insertions at carefully selected sites
3. Further optimization of zinc coordination chemistry

**Key Approaches:**

- **Rational introduction of disulfide bonds and salt bridges**
    - Engineered disulfide bonds enhance the rigidity of the protein backbone, reducing thermal denaturation without significantly compromising flexibility required for catalysis[2][4].
    - The rational addition of salt bridges, especially in networked arrangements, can strengthen overall protein structure and resist thermal disruption. However, the effectiveness of salt bridges can depend on local protein environment (e.g., pKa shifts, solvent exposure)[2][4].
    - The combination of these modifications has been shown to increase both thermostability (measured by half-life at elevated temperature) and may occasionally increase catalytic activity at moderate temperatures[2][4].

- **Directed evolution, rational and semi-rational design**
    - **Directed evolution** creates random mutations and screens large libraries for thermostable variants. While less targeted, it can find unexpected stabilizing mutations[3].
    - **Rational design** leverages structural data to identify key residues where modifications could enhance stability. This includes targeting flexible loops, termini, or surface regions for site-specific disulfide/salt bridge engineering[3].
    - **Semi-rational design** combines knowledge of mutation hotspots with limited randomization. It is often used to introduce stabilizing motifs like proline substitutions, or optimize networks of ionic interactions[3].
