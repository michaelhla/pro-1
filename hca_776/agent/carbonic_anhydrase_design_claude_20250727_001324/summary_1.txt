

# Carbonic Anhydrase II Engineering Summary

## Objective
Design more stable variants of carbonic anhydrase II (CA II) to improve thermal stability and acid resistance while maintaining catalytic activity for carbon capture applications.

## Key Research Findings
1. **Stability Enhancement Strategies**:
   - Disulfide bond engineering (A23C/L203C with C206S) increases thermal stability (+6.6 kcal/mol)
   - Surface electrostatics optimization improves acid stability
   - Hydrophobic core packing optimization increases overall stability
   - Proline introduction in loops enhances rigidity
   - Multiple point mutations can work additively for stabilization

2. **Critical Regions**:
   - Active site residues (Y7, N62, H64, N67, Q92) for proton transfer network
   - Zinc binding residues (H94, H96, H119) essential for catalysis
   - Surface-exposed residues important for acid stability
   - Loop regions amenable to rigidification

## Mutations Tested
Multiple design variants were tested with various mutations:

1. **First comprehensive design** - Rosetta score: 325.38 (worse than WT)
   - A23C/L203C/C206S + multiple additional mutations (V135P, N178R, A65R, L223F, A53I, S217D, V121I)

2. **Disulfide bond design** - Rosetta score: -300.38
   - A23C mutation

3. **Novel stability design** - Rosetta score: -304.17
   - K106R mutation (conservative surface change)

4. **Thermoacid stable variant** - Rosetta score: -232.07
   - D243E and K196R mutations

5. **Multi-surface variant** - Rosetta score: -278.89
   - C206K and C209R mutations (surface electrostatics)

6. **Strategic design** - Rosetta score: -279.17
   - V135I, T199I, C206K mutations

## Structural Analysis
- Wild-type CA II: Rosetta score -305.47
- Secondary structure: 17.3% helix, 26.5% sheet, 56.2% loop
- Compact structure with good radius of gyration (17.19 Å)
- Catalytic site geometry is critical to maintain

## Challenges and Results
- Maintaining the same or better Rosetta score while introducing stabilizing mutations proved difficult
- Most variants showed worse Rosetta scores than wild-type (-305.47)
- The best performing variant had a Rosetta score of -304.17 with a conservative K106R mutation
- No variant achieved the target of significantly improving on the wild-type score while maintaining structure

## Next Steps
1. Focus on more conservative mutations with established benefits
2. Combine A23C/L203C disulfide with fewer additional mutations
3. Explore computational optimization of surface charge patterns
4. Test additional variants with loop stabilization strategies
5. Consider directed evolution approaches alongside rational design
