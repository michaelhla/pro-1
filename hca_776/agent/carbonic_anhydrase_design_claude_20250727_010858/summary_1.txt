# Carbonic Anhydrase Engineering Project Summary

## Project Objective
Design a more stable carbonic anhydrase variant with increased thermal stability and acid tolerance while maintaining catalytic activity for carbon capture applications.

## Baseline Structure Analysis
- **Original sequence:** Human carbonic anhydrase II (hCA II), 260 residues
- **Baseline Rosetta score:** -305.46 REU
- **Secondary structure composition:** 17.3% helix, 26.5% sheet, 56.2% loop
- **Key catalytic residues:** Y7, N62, H64, N67, Q92 (proton transfer network)
- **Zinc binding residues:** H94, H96, H119 (essential for catalysis)

## Literature Research Findings
1. **Thermostability strategies:**
   - Disulfide bridge introduction (can increase melting temperature by 14°C)
   - Proline substitution in flexible surface loops
   - Compact protein cores with increased hydrophobicity
   - Ion pair networks from thermophilic organisms

2. **Acid tolerance insights:**
   - CA IX maintains structural integrity down to pH 2.0 and activity at pH 3.0
   - Acidic surface residues lower pI, enabling function at pH <6.0
   - Strategic surface modifications with acidic amino acids

3. **Domain engineering approaches:**
   - Small thermostable domains from extremophiles (e.g., Sso7d, Sac7d)
   - Strategic insertions into permissive "hotspots" away from active sites
   - Loop engineering in surface-exposed regions

## Most Promising Design: CA_Stabilized
- **Sequence modification:** Strategic insertion of a helical segment with acidic residues (EAIKKLKEAEEDEEGEDEEDD) into a surface loop
- **Rosetta score:** -46.67 REU (improved from original -305.46)
- **Structural integrity:** Very compact structure with good quality assessment
- **Catalytic site RMSD:** 0.097 (excellent preservation of catalytic geometry)
- **Secondary structure:** 16.7% helix, 24.5% sheet, 58.9% loop
- **Design rationale:** The focused insertion provides:
  1. Increased helical content for thermostability
  2. Acidic residues for low pH tolerance
  3. Minimal disruption to core enzyme structure

## Conclusions
1. Small, strategic insertions outperformed large domain fusions in this case
2. The insertion of acidic, helical segments provided the best balance of stability improvement and catalytic preservation
3. Future work could combine this approach with disulfide engineering or targeted surface mutations

The CA_Stabilized design represents a promising candidate for experimental validation, featuring significantly improved predicted stability while maintaining the structural integrity of the catalytic machinery.
