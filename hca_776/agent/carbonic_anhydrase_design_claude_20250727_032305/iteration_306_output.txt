Among the most **stable protein domains** for sequence insertions—used in biotechnology, protein engineering, or fusion constructs—are **zinc finger domains** (especially C2H2 type), certain **beta-barrel domains**, and the **cold shock domain**. **Zinc Finger Domain (C2H2 Type):**

**Beta Barrel Domain:**
- **Stability:** Classic β-barrel folds such as the **SH3 domain** or small **immunoglobulin-like domains** show high stability, good folding yields, and insertional tolerance.
- **Example:** SH3 domain from Src kinase (see PDB: 1SHG); Immunoglobulin V domain (e.g., PDB: 1IGT).

  The sequence forms a 5-stranded beta-barrel; loop insertions are often tolerated on RT or n-Src loops.

**Tetraneedle Domain:**  
This term is not widely established in structural biology literature. It might refer to a domain with four protruding strands (akin to certain beta-propeller or zinc finger arrangements), but no canonical sequence or fold with this name is recognized in major structural databases or reviews.

**Cold Shock Domain (CSD):**
- **Stability:** The cold shock domain is a highly conserved, stable 5-stranded beta-barrel found in both bacteria (CspA, E. coli) and eukaryotes (YB-1) and is known for its tolerance to glycine-rich loop insertions.
- **Example:** E. coli CspA (PDB: 1MJC)
- **Consensus/Representative Sequence:**

  The **GFGFI** motif is highly conserved and critical for function and folding. Surface loops can be targeted for insertions.
