# Carbonic Anhydrase Engineering Summary

## Main Objective
Design more stable carbonic anhydrase variants while maintaining catalytic activity to improve carbon capture applications.

## Original Protein Analysis
- Original sequence: 260 amino acids, starting with "MSHHWGYGKH..."
- Rosetta energy score: -304.55 REU
- Secondary structure: 17.3% helix, 26.5% sheet, 56.2% loop
- Key catalytic residues: Y7, N62, H64, N67, Q92 (proton transfer network)
- Zinc binding residues: H94, H96, H119 (essential for catalysis)

## Design Strategies Explored
1. **C-terminal domain fusion** with B. caldolyticus CspB cold shock protein
   - Added 66-aa CspB with GGGGSGGGGSGGGGS linker
   - Resulted in poor stability (Rosetta score: 1557.96 REU)
   - Catalytic site intact (RMSD: 0.091)

2. **Loop insertion strategy** with P. furiosus rubredoxin
   - Inserted 54-aa rubredoxin at position 169 with GGS linkers
   - Better stability (Rosetta score: 343.39 REU)
   - Excellent structural quality assessment
   - Catalytic site preserved (RMSD: 0.129)
   - More promising design with very compact structure

## Literature-Based Insights
- Successful domain fusions require careful linker design
- Thermostable domains from thermophiles provide stability benefits
- Small stable domains like rubredoxin (54 aa) are good candidates
- Strategic loop insertions can enhance stability without disrupting function
- Disulfide bonds in surface loops could further enhance stability

## Current Status
The rubredoxin insertion design shows the most promise, offering:
- Excellent structural quality
- Maintained catalytic residue positions
- Better Rosetta score than the C-terminal fusion
- Compact structure beneficial for stability

## Next Steps
1. Refine the rubredoxin insertion design with optimized linkers
2. Explore introducing strategic disulfide bonds

4. Test pH and thermal stability computationally

The insertion of the thermostable rubredoxin domain appears more effective than C-terminal fusion for enhancing carbonic anhydrase stability while preserving catalytic function.
