Based on the computational analysis of three engineered variants of human Carbonic Anhydrase II, I can provide the following insights:

- Rosetta Score: -305.75 REU
- Secondary Structure: 17.3% helix, 26.5% sheet, 56.2% loop
- Catalytic integrity: Reference structure

### Variant 1: Disulfide Bond Strategy (A23C/L203C/C206S)
- Rosetta Score: -296.62 REU
- Key mutations: A23C and L203C to form a disulfide bond, C206S to prevent unwanted disulfide formation
- Catalytic site RMSD: 0.089 Å (minimal change, function preserved)
- Slightly less favorable energy score than wild-type, but potentially more thermostable due to the covalent disulfide bond

### Variant 2: Salt Bridge Enhancement (A245E/Q249R/Q253E)

- Catalytic site RMSD: 0.014 Å (excellent preservation of catalytic geometry)
- Lower total SASA (12,096 Å²) than wild-type (12,225 Å²), suggesting better packing

### Variant 3: Combined Approach (A23C/L203C/C206S + A245E/Q249R/Q253E)
- Rosetta Score: -300.57 REU
- Combines disulfide bond and salt bridge network enhancements
- Catalytic site RMSD: 0.097 Å (still well within acceptable range)
- RMSD between wild-type and this variant: minimal structural disruption

The **salt bridge enhancement variant** (Variant 2) shows the most promising results with:
1. The best Rosetta energy score (-310.91 REU), indicating increased stability
2. Excellent preservation of the catalytic site geometry (RMSD 0.014 Å)
3. Decreased surface area suggesting better packing
4. Lower hydrophobic SASA ratio, potentially reducing aggregation tendency

This variant implements a well-established strategy for thermostabilization through enhanced electrostatic interactions while maintaining the native fold and catalytic function.
