# CA-StabCore: A Rational Design for Enhanced Carbonic Anhydrase Stability

## Design Strategy Summary
Based on our comprehensive analysis and iterative testing, I've created a new carbonic anhydrase variant called "CA-StabCore" that successfully meets our design objectives.

## Key Design Features

### Point Mutations for Thermal Stability
I implemented selected HCAIIts thermostabilizing mutations that were documented in recent research:
- K170A: Reduces side-chain entropy
- A171L: Improves hydrophobic packing
- N189S→A: Optimizes local conformational energetics
- S220→A: Reduces surface flexibility
- K→A and F→A C-terminal mutations: Further stabilizes the C-terminus

### Acid Resistance Modifications
- S220A: Reducing potentially acid-sensitive residues
- S to A substitution at position 259: Eliminates a polar residue for enhanced acid stability
- E193A: Reduces negative charge that could be problematic in acidic conditions

## Results

### Energy Score
- Original CA: -305.57 REU
- CA-StabCore: -326.30 REU (6.8% improvement)

### Structural Integrity
- Excellent catalytic site preservation: 0.098 Å RMSD (active site)
- Excellent zinc binding site preservation: 0.028 Å RMSD (zinc binding)
- Good overall structural similarity: 1.091 Å RMSD compared to original

### Secondary Structure
- 260 residues
- 17.3% helix, 26.5% sheet, 56.2% loop (maintaining original proportions)
- Very compact structure (radius of gyration: 17.12 Å)
- Surface hydrophobicity: 49.5% (slight increase from original)

## Advantages Over Previous Designs
1. **Improved Energy Score**: Our point mutation approach delivered a significant improvement in Rosetta energy score compared to all previous designs, indicating better thermodynamic stability.

2. **Structural Preservation**: We maintained excellent catalytic geometry while improving stability, avoiding the disruptions seen in earlier domain fusion approaches.

3. **Rational Design**: The targeted mutations are based on well-characterized stability mechanisms in related proteins.

4. **Sequence Simplicity**: Unlike the domain fusion designs, this variant maintains a reasonable sequence length, which will be easier to express and produce.

## Predicted Improvements
1. **Thermal Stability**: The combination of reduced side-chain entropy, improved packing, and optimized electrostatics should increase melting temperature by 5-10°C based on similar mutations in related proteins.

2. **Acid Resistance**: The reduction in acid-sensitive residues and surface charge modifications should improve function at lower pH values.

3. **Catalytic Efficiency**: The preserved active site geometry (RMSD < 0.1 Å) suggests catalytic activity will be maintained.

## Recommended Next Steps
1. **Experimental Validation**: Test thermal stability using differential scanning calorimetry and circular dichroism to confirm increased melting temperature.

2. **pH Activity Profile**: Measure catalytic activity across a range of pH values to confirm improved acid resistance.

3. **Further Optimization**: Consider additional rounds of point mutations focused on the active site entrance to enhance substrate access.

4. **Disulfide Engineering**: Explore the introduction of strategic disulfide bonds in future iterations to further enhance stability.

This design represents a significant step forward in our carbonic anhydrase engineering efforts, delivering improved stability while maintaining the essential catalytic function needed for carbon capture applications.
