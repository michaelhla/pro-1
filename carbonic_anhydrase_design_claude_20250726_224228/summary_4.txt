
# Carbonic Anhydrase Stability Engineering Summary

## Objective
Design thermostable variants of human carbonic anhydrase II (hCA2) while maintaining catalytic function.

## Key Findings from Literature
- Surface residue substitutions (hydrophobic→hydrophilic) enhance thermostability through increased hydrogen bonding
- Salt bridge formation can rigidify structure and improve thermostability
- Disulfide bonds can stabilize flexible loop regions
- Key flexible regions in hCA2: loops 1 (residues 22-33) and 2 (residues 55-67)

## Designs and Testing Results

### Wild-type hCA2 (Baseline)
- Rosetta Energy Score: -305.08 REU
- Surface hydrophobicity: 48.6%
- Catalytic site integrity: Reference baseline
- Secondary structure: 17.3% helix, 26.5% sheet, 56.2% loop

### Design 1: 5-Mutation Variant (A65T, L100H, L224S, L240P, A248T)
- Strategy: Surface hydrophobic to hydrophilic substitutions
- Rosetta Energy Score: -269.12 REU
- Surface hydrophobicity: 48.5%
- RMSD from wild-type: 0.205Å
- Catalytic site RMSD: 0.053Å (well preserved)
- Assessment: Maintains overall fold and catalytic function; slightly higher energy than wild-type

### Design 2: 8-Mutation Variant (5 mutations + S44R, S139E, A70D)
- Strategy: Combined hydrophobic→hydrophilic substitutions with salt bridge formation
- Rosetta Energy Score: 138.06 REU (significantly higher, indicating instability)
- Surface hydrophobicity: 49.0%
- RMSD from wild-type: 0.938Å
- Catalytic site RMSD: 0.192Å
- Assessment: Destabilized structure; salt bridge approach was unsuccessful

### Design 3: Novel Disulfide Bond Design (S56C, K128C, S174C, L178C)
- Strategy: Introduction of two disulfide bonds to stabilize flexible loops
- Mutations target loop 2 (S56C) connected to position 128, and a second disulfide between positions 174-178
- This approach is novel and distinct from the literature-reported 23-203 disulfide

## Current Status
- Testing of the disulfide bond design is in progress
- The 5-mutation design shows the most promise thus far
- The salt bridge approach (Design 2) was destabilizing

## Next Steps
1. Complete testing of the disulfide bond design
2. Compare structural properties and stability metrics across all designs
3. Consider combined approaches using the most effective elements from each design
4. Explore additional stabilization strategies such as proline substitutions in flexible loops
