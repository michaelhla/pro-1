**Circular permutation (CP)** is a versatile protein engineering strategy in which the natural N- and C-termini of a protein are joined—often by a peptide linker—and new termini are introduced at a different location in the sequence. This rearrangement can yield variants with altered folding, stability, activity, or substrate specificity[1][4]. CP has been used extensively in protein engineering, including for enzymes like **carbonic anhydrase**, to probe structure-function relationships or optimize biochemical properties.

### Circular Permutation Strategies for Carbonic Anhydrase Engineering

**1. Design Principles**
- Identify regions where introduction of new termini is unlikely to disrupt folding or function, often within flexible loops or solvent-exposed regions.
- Select sites for permutation using structural data to avoid secondary structure elements or key catalytic/structural residues[1][4].

**2. Molecular Techniques**
- **Join the original termini:** Fuse the native N- and C-termini using a *flexible peptide linker* (e.g., GSGGG or DKS linkers)[2][4].
- **Create new termini:** Break the sequence at the chosen site to generate a linear permutant with new N- and C-ends[1][4].
- **Gene construction:** This can be achieved by molecular cloning, either traditional (using restriction enzyme/ligation) or newer methods such as *Restriction Free (RF) cloning*, which enables rapid, seamless construction of permuted genes[3].
- *Random CP library generation* is possible by transposon insertion, exonuclease digestion, or random PCR breaks followed by re-ligation[2].
